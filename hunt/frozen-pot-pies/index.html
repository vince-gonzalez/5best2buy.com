Frozen Pot Pies Worth the Hunt

A grocery-freezer pot pie is a thin gravy-flavored paste under a cracker-crust lid, sized for one and forgettable. A real one is built on scratch pastry and actual pieces of chicken or beef in a proper sauce, then frozen unbaked so it goes into your oven and comes out like it was made that afternoon. These small bakeries ship them frozen, packed in dry ice.

Straight Answers
Frozen Pot Pies FAQ
Do I bake a frozen pot pie from frozen or thaw it first?

Most ship unbaked and are meant to go into the oven from frozen — thawing a raw pastry pie makes the bottom crust gummy. Bake at the temperature on the box until the crust is deep golden and the filling bubbles in the center, which takes longer than a thawed pie. Tent the edges with foil if they brown too fast.

How is a bakery pot pie different from a grocery-store one?

Scale and ingredients. A bakery pie uses real scratch pastry and actual pieces of meat and vegetables in a made sauce; the mass-market version leans on a thin, heavily-salted gravy and a minimal crust to hit a price. You taste the crust and the filling in a real one instead of mostly salt.

How many people does one pot pie feed?

It depends on size — a personal 6-inch pie serves one or two, while a 9- or 10-inch pie feeds four to six as a main with a salad on the side. Check the size on each product page; several makers offer both a single-serve and a family size.

How long will a frozen pot pie keep?

Kept solidly frozen, an unbaked pot pie generally keeps a few months without losing much quality; check the maker's guidance. Get it into your freezer as soon as it arrives. Once you've baked it, refrigerate leftovers and eat them within a few days rather than refreezing.
